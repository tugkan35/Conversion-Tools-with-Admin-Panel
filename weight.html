What is Weight?

Weight is the force with which an object is pulled toward the Earth (or another planet) due to gravity.

In simple words:

  • Weight tells us how heavy something feels, and it depends on both the mass of the object and the strength of gravity.

The formula for weight is:

Weight (W)=Mass (m)*Gravity (g)

Where:

  • Mass (m) is the amount of matter in an object (measured in kilograms, kg)
  • Gravity (g) is the acceleration due to gravity (on Earth, approximately 9.8m/s²)

For example:

  • If an object has a mass of 10 kg, its weight on Earth is:

W=10*9.8=98N (Newtons)

Important: Mass and weight are different:

  • Mass is constant everywhere.
  • Weight changes if gravity changes (for example, on the Moon, weight is less than on Earth).

Important Weight conversions :

1 Kilograms ( kg ) is equals to 1000 Grams ( g )

1 Pounds ( lb ) is equals to 454 Grams ( g )

1 Stones ( st ) is equals to 6,4 Kilograms ( kg )
